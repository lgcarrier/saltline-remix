## Capture and performance hooks worth preserving

At `original.html:6180`:

- `window.__frames`, `__ready`, `__fatal`, `__errors`, `__G`, `__stats`, and `__setShot` provide machine-readable status and capture controls.
- `cam=x,y,z,lookX,lookY,lookZ`, `fov`, and `exposure` control framing; `__setShot` can update them without rebooting. `cam:""` clears the override.
- `seed` controls the shared RNG; `dt` overrides simulation timestep. Do not call this fully deterministic without checking sources of unseeded randomness and real time. The context still records real time, and other modules can use `Math.random()`.
- The game calls `game.start({skipIntro:true})` for truthy `shot` or `autostart`; `noweapon` can hide the view model.
- `__ready` becomes true after the second animation loop. This signals initial frames, **not completion of all validation**.
- `__stats()` reports renderer stats, smoothed FPS, and game state. It is a snapshot, not a benchmark distribution.
- The benchmark flies along the authored cinematic path over a 30-second cycle, skips its first two seconds of timing samples, keeps up to 3,000 samples, and reports mean, median, p99 frame time, drawing-buffer dimensions, GPU, DPR, current quality, renderer stats, and errors (`original.html:6180–6187`). This is a much better transferable measurement design than publishing triangle counts alone.
- Weapon close-ups and pose controls survive at `original.html:6052`, search `window.__setShot=u=>`. Modes include side/optic/muzzle/hand views and reload/fire states. FX can freeze a chosen explosion age at `original.html:5926`, search `fxage`. These afford repeatable assessment of motion-dependent art.

### QA traps in the supplied artifact

1. **Use `?bench=1`, not bare `?bench`.** `Object.fromEntries(new URLSearchParams(location.search))` produces an empty string for a bare flag. The benchmark condition is `!!i.bench || location.hash==="#bench"`; consequently `?bench` alone is false. The README's example is misleading. `#bench` also works. `?shot=1`, `?autostart=1`, and `?noaudio=1` avoid the analogous truthiness issue. This is a static code finding; it was not exercised in a browser here.
2. **Specify quality when making reference images.** `quality:zy(i.q||(i.shot?"test":""))` defaults truthy shot mode to the `test` preset. That preset at `original.html:4178` uses 256px textures, a 1024px shadow map, 10 volumetric steps, no MSAA, no motion blur, and reduced rain. Use explicit `q=high` or `q=ultra` for visual comparisons, and record actual drawing-buffer size and quality. Do not mistake a test preset capture for the game's visual ceiling.
3. **No error plus ready is more informative than ready alone, but still insufficient.** Modules can be stubbed after a logged error, or rendering can degrade to a fallback. Check `__errors`, `__fatal`, relevant `__G.<module>.stub`, expected subsystem counts, and actual renderer mode. A title screen with missing systems may remain interactive.
4. **A cinematic performance route is not a full combat benchmark.** The benchmark sets player movement disabled and follows a camera path. Additional representative combat/reload/effects loads are needed before claiming whole-game frame-rate stability.
5. **Build, showcase, visual, gameplay, and device checks are separate evidence.** A material showroom proves neither enemy navigation nor mission completion. A screenshot proves neither animation quality nor audio synchronization. A two-frame boot proves neither.

## Recommended reusable skill workflow

This is a derived workflow, not a reconstructed historical transcript:

1. **Define a compact vertical slice and concrete target images.** Specify what the player does, the beginning/end condition, supported device/input, art direction, reference frames, and a frame-time budget. Match the user's ambition while interpreting “AAA quality” as explicit visual/game-feel targets, not a promise of a complete AAA production.
2. **Write the spatial and subsystem contract before parallel asset work.** Set axes/units, player dimensions, world bounds, lighting/exposure convention, material channel convention, update/time API, lifecycle/disposal, event payloads, and the level data exported to gameplay. Record ownership and allowed edit areas. Supply small working stubs so modules can integrate immediately.
3. **Build a playable loop and one representative scene first.** A target camera should exercise scale, silhouette, hero materials, foreground/midground/background, lighting, wetness, and HUD/weapon framing. Lock the shared look before multiplying content. Procedural generation should express a physical construction story, not scatter arbitrary noise or primitive clutter.
4. **Delegate by separable subsystem, with integration owned centrally.** Rendering/materials, level/props, characters/weapon, audio/gameplay are candidate workstreams when capacity allows. Do not hardcode “twelve agents.” Every handoff returns interface adherence, runnable isolated view, metrics, and integration caveats. Shared configuration stays with the integration owner.
5. **Provide isolated showrooms and deterministic capture controls while building.** A material gallery, prop turntable, enemy locomotion/attack cycle, weapon pose gallery, FX age control, and audio audition path reduce iteration cost. Keep capture resolution, quality, exposure, camera, and seed visible in evidence.
6. **Use two different review passes.** The implementation reviewer checks code, contracts, errors, fallbacks, resource counts, gameplay progression, and performance. A visual critic receives the target references and output frames/clips without the implementation narrative, then identifies the largest perceptual failures. Treat judgments as findings to test, not numerical proof of parity. This captures the valuable idea in the supplied screenshots without asserting that their exact history is independently proven.
7. **Repair by perceptual impact and run regression checks.** Prioritize composition/scale, light and exposure, material response, contact/grounding, animation and weapon feedback, then smaller detail. Validate frame-time and interaction after art changes. Avoid optimizing toward a triangle-count target.
8. **Finish with a verifiable completion record.** Include a complete start-to-success path, failure/restart/pause/input checks, reference-quality captures, stress-load performance statistics, audio verification, and observed device/browser coverage. Report what remains unverified. Preserve the source plus reproducible run/capture commands.

The strongest lesson is not “find a magical short prompt.” It is that a short external brief can initiate a long internal engineering loop with decomposition, contracts, inspection tools, and iterative correction. The available source substantiates those capabilities; it cannot independently establish all claims about how this particular run was conducted.
