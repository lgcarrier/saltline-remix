# Saltline rendering and procedural-surface audit

Scope: static inspection of `/Users/lgcarrier/Developer/saltline-remix/original.html`, not the remixed `index.html`. Original is a bundled artifact: much custom JS shares very long lines (especially 4731, 5281, 5440, 5461, 5648, 5812). The anchors and function names below make evidence findable. No browser execution or performance measurement was performed in this audit. Screenshot quality, hardware FPS, generation history, and “AAA parity” are not proven by this source inspection.

## What the code establishes

### Material generation

- **27 distinct surface shader definitions**, `Tn.asphalt` through `Tn.water`, original.html:4820–5263. They are asphalt, concrete, brick, epoxy, paint, hazard, hull, galv, metal, brushed, rust, diamond, corrugated, chainlink, gunmetal, polymer, carbon, fabric, armor, wood, plastic, tarp, cardboard, rope, tire, glass, water.
- **29 parameterized texture-set recipes and 48 named material definitions**, in `c3` and `l1` at 5281. For example anodized reuses the gunmetal shader with a different parameter/seed; glass_dirty reuses glass. Therefore do not conflate surface programs, baked sets, and named material instances.
- GLSL 3 MRT outputs are declared at 4737. `emit` at 4778–4782 packs sqrt-encoded albedo/alpha, ORM (R=AO, G=roughness, B=metalness), and height encoded across R/G. `Gg` at 5440 allocates `count:3`, renders one surface pass, then performs **two texture-copy passes plus a normal conversion pass**. The albedo copy squares the encoding back to linear. “One GPU pass” describes generation of the three fields, not the entire ready-to-use material pipeline.
- Normal conversion at 5270–5277 samples cardinal and diagonal height neighbors. Normal strength is physically scaled in `Gg`: `bump * .001 * resolution / tileDimension`. Recipes express tile size in meters and bump in millimeters; the corrugated recipe uses `.762 × 2.286 m` and 19 mm bump. This is a valuable contract: texture resolution changes must not change physical material relief.
- Most expensive noise is baked, cached by set, and reused through inexpensive texture sampling. Bake render targets and shader materials are released after an idle interval; retained textures have mipmaps and anisotropy (up to 8). `stats()` exposes sets, texture MB, generation milliseconds, decal MB (5440). Hero resolution may differ from common sets.
- Hash uses PCG-like integer mixing; periodic gradient noise and Voronoi support tiling (4742–4766). There is macro/micro frequency separation, not a uniform noise overlay. Damage has causal layers: clustered chips, primer/substrate, rust stain, downward runs (4793–4817; paint 4890–4940). Preserve cleaner regions so damage has structure.
- Runtime material injection adds world-space macro variation, directional wetness, near-ground splash bands, and close-range water beading (5282–5437). Up-facing surfaces receive more rain; undersides are protected; interiors use a lower wetness factor. Roughness and normal changes accompany albedo darkening. Wetness is not simply “make everything glossy.”
- Anti-aliasing is addressed in assets too: normal variance increases roughness to reduce specular shimmer; subpixel chainlink shifts alpha thresholds and eventually dithers coverage (5400 area). This is more reusable than copying a grain/bloom preset.

### Lighting and render pipeline

- The scene is built on bundled Three.js, rather than an engine built entirely from scratch. Renderer customizations are substantial: `Hg` and `jy` at 4731; shader markers `SALTLINE_SKY` (4194), `SALTLINE_SHADOW` (4283), `SALTLINE_POST` (4336).
- Procedural sky has a deliberate composition: cool storm deck, warm gap toward the sun, density-gradient cloud relief, and lightning. The visible sun disk is omitted from environment capture so the directional sun does not get counted twice. PMREM capture clamps bright environment values to reduce fireflies (4194–4283). Indoor materials receive a separate captured probe after level construction (4731, `captureInteriorProbe` / `onLevelReady`). Lighting is matched across sky, surfaces, particles, reflections, and atmosphere.
- Custom contact-hardening shadows patch Three's shader chunk: blocker search, Vogel disk samples, receiver-plane depth bias, penumbra in world units, and edge fade (4283–4336). The patch first checks expected chunk structure, and renderer startup tests a lit/shadowed cube; failed shadow compilation restores stock PCF (4731). This is a version-sensitive technique requiring a pinned dependency and explicit fallback, not a universal baseline.
- Advanced path renders HDR scene color/depth, half-resolution AO and shadow-aware volumetrics, bilateral blur/upsampling, optional camera motion blur, multi-scale bloom/streaks, exposure adaptation, grading, then FXAA/sharpen/grain/dither (4336–4731). AO is reduced in brightly lit areas to avoid dirty sunlit halos (4478–4482).
- Grade blends ACES/AgX, gives cool shadows/warm highlights and lifted blacks, then converts to sRGB once (4671–4699). HDR lighting and linear compositing precede tone mapping. Copying arbitrary effects without preserving this order will yield a different image.
- First-person viewmodel uses a separate FOV/layer and compressed depth range. AO/motion passes exempt near depth values; world scene and viewmodel environment handling are explicit (4731; 4462; 4492). Separate viewmodel rendering is part of the polished appearance and avoids weapon/world interference.
- Renderer errors are categorized by shader marker, advanced initialization/frame failures fall back to forward rendering, and startup compilation prewarms materials (4731). Robustness is observable and designed in, not merely implied by a successful build.

### Water, rain, and cross-system agreement

- Wet apron uses a **shared CPU-generated world-space data map** with puddle height, dampness, oil and wear (`Rb`, `U1`, `Li` at 5461). Shader adds detail textures, slab expansion joints, tar, cracks, markings, polished lanes, and separate dry/damp/soaked/water transitions (5500–5620).
- A reflected camera with an oblique clip plane renders a planar reflection, at a quality-dependent resolution; reflective surfaces are hidden during that pass (5648). Roughness-dependent mip level and stretched multi-tap sampling turn wet ground into blurred sheen while puddles are sharper (5619–5640). This is not ray tracing, and has the cost/limitations of a second scene render.
- **Important precision caveat:** CPU `isPuddle` at 5812 uses the bilinearly sampled base map with depth > .015. The visual shader uses bicubic sampling plus extra shoreline noise/detail and a smooth depth ramp (5461, 5471, 5504, 5569 onward). They share the major puddle layout, but “splashes exactly wherever pixels show water” overstates the implementation. Skill lesson: define a common semantic surface query and quantify acceptable disagreement, rather than promise pixel identity.
- Splash FX consume `ground.isPuddle` on player step/land and impact (5926). Ground exposes wetness, puddle depth, sea height, rain and reflection controls (5812). Gameplay/material response is a shared contract.
- Ocean has coarse-distance grid spacing, four shared displacement waves, detail-wave normals, and a matching CPU sea-height query (`B1`, `Lb` at 5648; mesh at 5699). Rain/splash instances are shader-driven and scaled by quality; rain respects interior boxes/skylights (5812).

### Performance and verification hooks

- Quality presets at 4178 explicitly trade render scale, MSAA, shadow resolution, texture resolution, volumetric steps, AO, planar reflections, motion blur and rain. The low profile disables planar reflections/AO/motion blur. This is perceptual budgeting by subsystem, not reducing all fidelity equally.
- Dynamic resolution at 4731 uses an initial settling delay, a window of up to 45 frames/~900 ms, downshift above 21.5 ms, faster cuts for very slow frames, and slow upgrades below 17.4 ms with exponential backoff after degradation. Render scale bottoms at .6; repeated >26 ms at the floor drops the quality tier. Pixel counts have tier budgets (1.0/1.5/2.3/3.7 million). UI remains at display resolution.
- It samples wall-clock frame intervals, not GPU timer-query timings. Therefore source proves adaptive behavior, not that the bottleneck is GPU-only or that a target FPS is attained.
- `shot`/`show` modes disable dynamic resolution, providing more stable comparison captures (4731). `window.__stats` returns resolution, scale, calls, triangles, program/texture/geometry counts, FPS and game state (4731, 6180). Record these with screenshots; a pretty frame at hidden low resolution is not quality evidence.
- The renderer resets counters before prepasses and reports accumulated render work, so the screenshot's “2.9 million triangles drawn for this frame” should not be treated as unique mesh triangles or a universal budget. A static screenshot cannot establish frame pacing.

## Transfer into a Codex skill

1. Begin with a bounded playable slice and a named set of representative camera views; set meter scale, target display resolution and hardware/frame-time budgets before detailing assets.
2. Lock one coherent lighting/material reference scene before broadening content. Add a material lab (plane, sphere, grazing-angle test, dry/wet control) and isolate individual systems through URL/config switches.
3. Specify cross-team contracts early: material names + physical scale, renderer layers, surface query schema, camera FOVs, update timing, quality settings, disposal and debug interfaces. Share wetness/ground data instead of independent visual and audio guesses.
4. Bake costly static procedures; keep dynamic response separate. Spend geometry on silhouette/bevels/near-field form, texture fields on meso/micro detail, and world masks on non-repeating environmental wear.
5. Implement the minimum render chain that earns its frame-time cost. Preserve linear/HDR order; show per-pass toggles and a forward fallback. Do not mandate custom shader patches or all these effects for every game.
6. Validate visual quality through fixed-seed, fixed-camera comparisons and an independent screenshot review; validate gameplay and performance separately. Check temporal shimmer, exposure pumping, reflection edges and readability in motion, not only hero images.
7. Define quality degradation deliberately: protect input/HUD clarity, visible silhouettes and material identity; reduce costly reflections/volumetrics/shadows before indiscriminately deleting detail. Use hysteresis to prevent oscillation.
8. A polished slice is a reusable production approach; this artifact is not evidence that every genre, world scale or hardware target will reach AAA quality from the same settings.
